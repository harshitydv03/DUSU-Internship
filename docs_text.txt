
DUSU U N I V E R S I T Y O F D E L H I · E S T . 1 9 4 9 A Chronicle of Service &amp; Initiative A documentation of the Delhi University Students' Union across three tenures of Indian student politics - from its inauguration under Jawaharlal Nehru to the campus of today. About this record DUSU is the apex representative body of the University of Delhi, coordinating over 700,000 students across 91 affiliated colleges and 16 faculties - by most counts, the largest student union in the world. This chronicle traces its journey across three tenures, reading each era through two lenses: Core I - Service, the Union's day-to-day work of representation and welfare, and Core II - Initiative, the movements and campaigns it drove or was defined by. Core I - Service (representation, welfare, governance) Core II - Initiative (Movements, campaigns, and student initiatives)I T E N U R E O N E Founding &amp; the Making of a Democracy 1949 - 1990 DUSU is inaugurated in newly independent India, gradually democratises its own election system, and plays a significant role in upholding democratic participation during the Emergency, further shaping the character of student politics in India.C o r e I Service Building the institution and its representative machinery 1. 1947 Provisional committee formed Under V.K.R.V. Rao, then head of DU's Economics Department, presidents of college unions convene to draft the DUSU constitution. The committee's early meetings set out the founding logic of a central body that could speak for students across every affiliated college rather than leaving representation fragmented. This groundwork, done in the shadow of Partition and independence, would shape the Union's structure for decades to come. 2. 9 April 1949 DUSU inaugurated Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru formally inaugurates the Union, establishing it as DU's central representative body. His presence at the founding ceremony lends the new body immediate national visibility and situates it within the broader project of building democratic institutions in a newly independent India. The inauguration is remembered as the moment DUSU's founding mandate - to represent, organise and speak for DU's students - was formally set in motion. 3. 1954 First DUSU elections heldGajraj Bahadur Nagar becomes the Union's first elected president. The election establishes the precedent of a contested, ballot-based leadership rather than a nominated or appointed one, giving the Union democratic legitimacy from its earliest years. It also sets the annual electoral rhythm that DUSU has followed, with only occasional interruptions, ever since.4.  1960s-1980s WUS Health Centre RepresentationDUSU regularly raised student concerns regarding medical services at the World University Service (WUS) Health Centre, advocating for better healthcare facilities, medicines and improved access to medical consultation.5. Ongoing Office-bearer structure fixedPresident, Vice-President, Secretary and Joint Secretary established as the four central posts, with Central Councillors representing each college. This four-post model gives the Union a clear division of responsibility, from public representation to day-to-day administrative work, that has proved durable across seven decades. The Central Councillor layer ensures that even DU's smaller and more distant colleges retain a direct line into the Union's central decision-making.6. Ongoing Patron &amp; governance link The Vice-Chancellor of Delhi University is instituted as patron, formally anchoring DUSU within university governance. This arrangement gives the Union a recognised, institutional channel to the university's highest office rather than leaving student representation informal or ad hoc. It also means that DUSU's activities and elections are, at least nominally, conducted under the university's oversight. 7. Ongoing Union fund &amp; welfare subscription An annual student subscription, collected with college admission fees, is set up to fund welfare activity and union operations. The fund gives DUSU a predictable, institutionally-backed revenue base rather than dependence on ad hoc collections or external donors. Over time it comes to underwrite everything from small welfare grants to the logistics of the Union's annual election cycle. 8. 1972–73 Student Movement for Electoral ReformStudents across the University of Delhi advocated for reforms to the existing indirect election system, seeking greater democratic participation in the selection of DUSU office-bearers. Through sustained engagement with the University administration, this movement contributed to the adoption of the "one student, one vote" system in 1973, marking a significant milestone in the democratic evolution of DUSU. 9. 1973 Direct election adopted DU moves from indirect elections (colleges electing councillors who chose office-bearers) to "one student, one vote" - a structural reform led in large part by student agitation. The shift hands every enrolled student a direct say in choosing DUSU's central leadership, rather than filtering that choice through college councillors. It marks one of the earliest and most significant democratic reforms in the Union's institutional history, and the model has held with only minor modification since. 10. 1974–75 Arun Jaitley's presidency Elected from SRCC, Jaitley's tenure is remembered as consolidating the new direct-democracy framework of DUSU. His presidency comes to be seen as proof that the reformed, directly-elected structure could function smoothly and command wide student legitimacy. It also marks the beginning of a public career that would later extend into national politics and a long tenure as a senior cabinet minister. 11. 1977 Post-Emergency transition of leadership Vijay Goel (ABVP) is elected president, restoring normal representative functioning after the Emergency years. His victory signals a return to competitive, openly contested campus politics after a period in which dissent had been forced underground. The transition also reflects the wider national mood following the Emergency, as student and civic bodies across India resumed ordinary democratic functioning. 12.  1970s–1980s Library Hours &amp; Reading Room ExpansionThe Union represented students before the University to extend library timings, improve reading-room facilities and increase access during examinations.C o r e I I Initiative Movements, campaigns, and student initiatives 1. 1942 Roots in the Quit India Movement Students of Hindu College, St. Stephen's and Indraprastha College mobilise college-to-college for the release of jailed Congress leaders - the activist lineage DUSU would later inherit. Their informal, crosscollege coordination in 1942 prefigures the very structure of shared representation that DUSU would formalise seven years later. This early wave of student activism is often cited as the moral and organisational precedent that gives DUSU's later movements their sense of historical continuity. 2. 1949 Uniting a post-Partition campus The new Union takes on the task of channelling post-independence, post-Partition student energy into organised, constructive activity. With Delhi absorbing large numbers of displaced students and families in the aftermath of Partition, DUSU's early work also involves helping integrate a campus reshaped by upheaval. This nation-building role gives the Union's founding years a purpose well beyond ordinary campus administration. 3. 1972-73 Campaign for Direct ElectionsDUSU played an important role in advocating for direct student elections through sustained student engagement and dialogue with the University administration. The movement ultimately contributed to the introduction of the "one student, one vote" system in 1973, strengthening democratic participation within the University. 4. 25-26 June 1975 Democratic Response during the EmergencyUnder the leadership of DUSU President Arun Jaitley, the Union organised peaceful student protests expressing concern over the declaration of the Emergency and its impact on democratic institutions. The episode is remembered as an important moment in DUSU's history of student participation in national democratic discourse. 5. 1975 Arrest under MISA Jaitley is arrested under the Maintenance of Internal Security Act and detained for roughly 19 months, becoming a symbol of student resistance to the Emergency. His prolonged detention without trial, alongside thousands of other political prisoners, draws attention to the Emergency's broader suppression of dissent. The episode later becomes a defining chapter in the personal and political narrative built around his subsequent public career. 6. 1975-77 Student Engagement during the EmergencyDuring the Emergency period, student leaders continued to promote democratic values and student representation despite restrictions on public political activity. This period reinforced DUSU's long-standing commitment to civic participation and constitutional principles, leaving a lasting influence on the culture of student leadership at the University.7. 1977 Democratic Restoration CampaignFollowing the end of the Emergency, DUSU leaders emphasised the restoration of democratic values, student participation and institutional accountability. The campaign reflected the broader national transition towards democratic governance and renewed student engagement. 8. 1982 Protest over assault on a Ramjas professor Students mobilise campus-wide against an attack on a Ramjas College faculty member. The protest draws participation from multiple colleges and political groups, briefly setting aside their usual rivalries in a shared demand for accountability. It reflects a recurring pattern in DUSU's history, in which incidents of violence against faculty or students prompt broad, cross-factional mobilisation. 9.  1965 &amp; 1971 -National Defence Relief Fund Collection Drives                                                                      During the Indo–Pakistan Wars of 1965 and 1971, DUSU mobilised students across the University of Delhi to contribute towards national relief and welfare efforts. The Union encouraged voluntary donations, fundraising campaigns and student participation in initiatives supporting the armed forces and war-affected civilians. 10. 1980s - Blood Donation &amp; Public Health Awareness CampsDUSU supported voluntary blood donation camps and health awareness programmes organised in collaboration with hospitals, voluntary organisations and university bodies. These initiatives encouraged students to participate in community service while promoting public health awareness on campus.II T E N U R E T W O Reservation, Reckoning &amp; Realignment 1990 -1999 C o r e I Service Holding representation together through crisis 1. 1990 Channelling student sentiment on Mandal As the V.P. Singh government moves to implement the Mandal Commission's OBC quota, DUSU becomes a formal channel through which student opposition is voiced to the administration. The Union's office-bearers find themselves mediating between an anxious student body and a university administration wary of the policy's implications for admissions and jobs. This role thrusts DUSU into the national spotlight in a way few campus bodies of the time experience. 2. 1991 Rajiv Goswami elected president Rajiv Goswami, who emerged as a prominent student leader during the Mandal Commission debate, was elected DUSU's first independent president since the introduction of direct elections. His victory reflected the changing political landscape of the period and demonstrated the significance of student leadership within the University's democratic process. 3. Through the decade Two-tier college representation maintained Central Councillors from each of DU's affiliated colleges continue to anchor DUSU's link to individual college unions. This layered structure ensures that even as national issues dominate headlines, the Union's day-to-day representative machinery keeps functioning at the college level. It gives DUSU a resilience that lets it absorb political shocks without losing its basic organisational continuity. 4. Through the decade Welfare fund &amp; subscription continued The annual union fund keeps supporting basic student welfare work even as campus politics grows more turbulent. Its continuity through a decade of agitation and shifting leadership shows how administrative functions can persist independently of the Union's more visible political battles. The fund remains one of the quieter, less-publicised threads holding DUSU's core service mandate together. 5. 1996–97 Rekha Gupta's presidency Gupta, who would go on to become Chief Minister of Delhi decades later, serves as DUSU president - representative continuity feeding into a much longer public career. Her tenure is a further example of DUSU functioning as an early proving ground for leaders who later rise to prominent public office. It reinforces the pattern, visible across the Union's history, of student politics acting as a training ground for state and national leadership. 6. Mid-1990s - Women's Safety &amp; Common Room FacilitiesDUSU consistently represented the concerns of women students before the University administration by advocating for improved campus security, better lighting, expanded women's common rooms, and basic amenities. These efforts reflected the Union's growing emphasis on creating a safer and more inclusive academic environment. 7. Through the decade Grievance redressal on hostel &amp; exam issues DUSU continues its core function of taking up hostel, fee and examination-related grievances with the university administration. These day-to-day representations rarely make headlines but remain the bread-and-butter work through which most students actually experience the Union. They also provide office bearers a steady channel for building goodwill independent of the decade's larger ideological battles. 8. Through the decade Constitutional continuity preserved Despite the decade's turbulence, the President-VP-Secretary-Joint Secretary structure and annual election cycle remain intact. This institutional stability allows the Union to keep functioning as a recognisable, predictable body even as the political temperature around it rises sharply. It is a reminder that DUSU's founding architecture proved sturdy enough to survive a genuinely disruptive period in campus politics. 9. Late-1990s - Career Counselling &amp; Employment GuidanceAs career opportunities diversified during the decade, DUSU facilitated career guidance sessions, competitive examination awareness programmes and interactions with professionals from various fields. These initiatives aimed to help students make informed academic and professional choices. 10. 1990s - Examination Result Correction AssistanceDUSU assisted students facing errors in examination results, mark sheets, migration certificates and re-evaluation procedures. By representing such cases before the University administration, the Union helped ensure timely resolution of academic and administrative grievances. C o r e I I Initiative Movements that defined the decade 1. 19 September 1990 Student Leadership during the Mandal DebateRajiv Goswami emerged as one of the prominent student voices during the national debate surrounding the implementation of the Mandal Commission recommendations. His leadership during this period made him a widely recognised figure in student politics and contributed to his subsequent election as DUSU President in 1991. 2. 1990 Student Mobilisation on Reservation PolicyThe implementation of the Mandal Commission recommendations generated extensive discussion and student participation across the University of Delhi. DUSU served as an important platform through which students expressed diverse viewpoints and engaged with issues relating to higher education, employment and public policy, reflecting the Union's representative role during a period of significant national debate. 3. 1990 National Debate on Reservation PolicyDevelopments at the University of Delhi formed an important part of the wider national discussion surrounding the implementation of the Mandal Commission recommendations. During this period, DUSU served as a representative platform through which students engaged with significant questions relating to education, employment and public policy.4. 1990s - Anti-Drug &amp; Substance Abuse Awareness CampaignsDUSU supported awareness campaigns highlighting the harmful effects of drug and substance abuse among students. Through seminars, public discussions and collaborative outreach programmes, the Union encouraged healthy lifestyles and responsible student behaviour. 5. Through the Decade Student Representation in a Changing Political LandscapeThe debates of the 1990s brought greater attention to issues of social representation, inclusion and public policy within university campuses. DUSU continued to provide a democratic platform where students from diverse backgrounds and perspectives participated in discussions, elections and representative processes, reflecting the evolving nature of student politics during the decade. 6. Late-1990s -Voter Awareness &amp; Democratic Participation CampaignsDUSU organised programmes encouraging students to understand democratic values, electoral participation and responsible citizenship. Through debates, awareness drives and interactive sessions, the Union strengthened civic engagement among first-time voters and university students. 7. Through the Decade Growing National SignificanceDUSU increasingly gained recognition as an important platform for student leadership, with several national political organisations taking greater interest in campus elections. The Union's prominence contributed to its reputation as an early training ground for future public representatives. 8. Late 1990s Rise of student leaders into party structures Several DUSU office-bearers of the decade move directly into their parties' state and national youth wings after their terms end. This direct pipeline gives ambitious student politicians a clear pathway from campus office to organised party structures. It reinforces DUSU's growing reputation as an incubator for the next generation of party workers and elected leaders. 9. 1990s - Environmental Awareness &amp; Campus Cleanliness DrivesIn collaboration with hospitals, voluntary organisations and university departments, DUSU promoted voluntary blood donation drives and basic health check-up camps. These initiatives encouraged social responsibility while improving awareness of preventive healthcare among students. 10. Through the Decade Campus Culture and Student EngagementCultural festivals, student societies and inter-college events continued to serve as important platforms for interaction, leadership development and student participation. These activities strengthened campus engagement and encouraged collaboration across the University community. III T E N U R E T H R E E Reform, Reach &amp; the Modern Union 2000 - 2024 C o r e I Service Governance, reform, and representation at scale 1. 2006 Extension of OBC Reservation in Higher EducationThe implementation of 27% reservation for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in higher education marked a significant national policy development. DUSU continued to provide a representative platform for student dialogue and engagement on issues relating to higher education and public policy.2. 2000s onward - Lyngdoh Committee Norms AdoptedDelhi University adopted the UGC-recommended Lyngdoh Committee guidelines governing student-union elections, introducing expenditure limits, candidate eligibility criteria and a code of conduct to promote transparency, fairness and responsible electoral practices. These reforms represented one of the most significant developments in the governance of DUSU elections since the introduction of direct voting in 1973. 3. 2011–15 Continuity of office-bearer service Successive presidents (including Arun Hooda, Anil Chaudhary and Mohit Nagar) keep the Union's core representative and welfare functions running annually. Their tenures, though less dramatic than earlier decades' upheavals, keep the Union's basic representative machinery ticking over reliably. This steady, less headline-grabbing period of service work is as much a part of DUSU's history as its more turbulent chapters. 4. 2016–2024 - Accessibility Support for Students with DisabilitiesDUSU advocated for improved accessibility across university campuses by encouraging the installation of ramps, accessible classrooms, assistive infrastructure and inclusive student services. These efforts reinforced the University's commitment to providing equal educational opportunities for students with disabilities.5. 2015-2024 -PG &amp; Rental Accommodation SupportWith an increasing number of outstation students enrolling at the University of Delhi, DUSU regularly assisted students facing accommodation-related challenges. The Union addressed concerns regarding affordable housing, tenant safety, rental disputes and basic living conditions while promoting greater awareness of safe off-campus accommodation. 6. 2010s-2024 - Internship &amp; Placement FacilitationRecognising the growing importance of employability, DUSU supported internship opportunities, placement awareness programmes and career-oriented workshops in collaboration with organisations, industry professionals and university departments. These efforts aimed to bridge the gap between academic learning and professional development. 7. 2020–21 Pandemic-era advocacy The Union takes up student concerns around online classes, examinations and fee relief through the COVID-19 disruption. Office-bearers press the administration on issues ranging from internet access for online learning to postponed practicals and financial hardship among students. This period tests DUSU's core service function under genuinely unprecedented, non-political circumstances. 8. 2022 Centenary-year engagement DUSU represents the student body during Delhi University's centenary commemorations, including a welcome for Olympic gold medallist Neeraj Chopra. The centenary year gives the Union a rare opportunity for celebratory, rather than adversarial, engagement with the university administration. Hosting a national sporting icon also lends the occasion added visibility beyond DU's own campus. 9. 2024 Election-cycle management DUSU's provisional candidate lists and election machinery for key posts continue functioning on schedule. The smooth administration of nominations, scrutiny and voting reflects how institutionalised the Union's electoral process has become over seventy-five years. It stands in contrast to earlier decades when elections were more frequently disrupted by agitation or administrative delay. 10. 2018-2024 - Mental Health &amp; Student Counselling AdvocacyRecognising the increasing importance of student well-being, DUSU promoted mental health awareness and encouraged the strengthening of counselling and psychological support services. Through discussions, awareness campaigns and engagement with university authorities, the Union highlighted emotional well-being as an essential component of student welfare. C o r e I I Initiative Campaigns, reform pushes, and mobilisation 1. 2010s Digital-era campaigning Candidates increasingly extend campus canvassing onto social media, changing how DUSU elections are fought and followed. Platforms such as Facebook, WhatsApp and later Instagram become central tools for reaching students beyond what door-to-door canvassing alone could achieve. This shift also makes DUSU campaigns more visible to a national audience following along online, well outside DU's own colleges. 2. 2019–20 Student Engagement on Contemporary Public IssuesDuring the national discussions surrounding the Citizenship Amendment Act, DUSU continued to serve as a platform through which students expressed diverse perspectives and participated in democratic dialogue on matters of public importance. 3. 2020-2021 - COVID-19 Student Relief &amp; Community SupportDuring the COVID-19 pandemic, DUSU coordinated relief efforts including the distribution of masks, sanitisers and essential supplies while assisting students facing accommodation, examination and financial challenges.4. 2015-2024 - Gender Sensitisation &amp; Self-Defence WorkshopsDUSU collaborated with university authorities, security agencies and student organisations to organise gender sensitisation programmes and self-defence workshops for students. These initiatives promoted awareness of gender equality, personal safety and the prevention of harassment on campus.5. 2016-2024 -Swachh Campus &amp; Plastic-Free Campus CampaignsInspired by national cleanliness and sustainability initiatives, DUSU encouraged students to participate in campus cleanliness drives, tree plantation activities and campaigns promoting reduced plastic usage. These programmes fostered environmental responsibility and sustainable campus practices.6. Aug 2025 Protest over an NCERT module  demonstration at the Faculty of Arts against a school-curriculum module on the Partition of India. The protest ties DUSU politics to an ongoing national debate over how school history curricula should represent Partition-era events. It illustrates how education-policy controversies well beyond DU's own syllabus can still become flashpoints for campus mobilisation. 7. 2023-2024 - Financial Literacy &amp; Career Readiness ProgrammesDUSU supported sessions on financial planning, resume building, interview preparation and workplace skills to help students transition successfully from university to professional careers. 8. 2022-2024 - Skill Development, Entrepreneurship &amp; Leadership ConclavesDUSU supported leadership summits, entrepreneurship workshops and skill-development programmes featuring policymakers, entrepreneurs, civil servants and industry experts. These initiatives provided students with opportunities to develop professional skills, leadership qualities and career awareness beyond the classroom.9. 2022-2024 - North Campus Safety &amp; Night Mobility InitiativeDUSU consistently highlighted concerns relating to inadequate street lighting, isolated routes, public transport accessibility and student security during late hours. Through meetings with university authorities, Delhi Police and civic agencies, the Union advocated for enhanced police patrolling, improved lighting, installation of CCTV cameras and safer last-mile connectivity. These efforts reflected DUSU's continued commitment to strengthening campus safety and creating a more secure environment for students attending evening classes, libraries and university events. 10. Ongoing DUSU as a national talent pipeline The Union continues to function as an early proving ground for figures who go on to hold office in state and national government. This role, visible in figures from Arun Jaitley to Rekha Gupta, remains one of the most consistent threads across all three tenures covered in this chronicle. It underscores DUSU's enduring significance well beyond the boundaries of campus politics alone.
